Constraint satis-what?


•
 
What are we doing now: You want to decide the value of lots of variables


given a bunch of possible values and certain constraints.


•
 
A lot of problems have this underlying structure such as scheduling different


teachers for lectures, what order to build a car in given limited resources,


deciding where to seat people at a dinner party who have very strong opinions


about each other, solving Sudoku boards, etc.


•
 
This is an NP-complete problem. Can make a lot of money for companies who


do things at a large scale by improving their schedules.
5


Constraint satis-what?


•
 
You need a set of variables, X={seat1, seat2, …, seat7}.


•
 
You need a domain for each variable,
 
D_seat1 = {Emily, Anne, …, Charlotte}.


•
 
You need constraints like C(Emily, Charlotte): Not adjacent, etc.


•
 
Doesn’t really matter how you represent this.


•
 
Intuition: Probably some of these constraints immediately rule out possibilities


(constraint propagation like AC-3). Then we can just try some assignments


and go back if we get stuck (backtrack search). We can also update


connected domains after making each move (forward checking).

Small problem


•
 
Basically with the heuristics you are
 
guaranteed


1 mistake.


•
 
With random choices, 4/6 times you will pick B


first or second which gives you 1/3+1/3=2/3


expected mistakes.


•
 
The two other cases are more tedious, but the


expected amount of mistakes is (4(2/3)+2(7/9))/


6=~0.704.


•
 
1/3 (A and C, same color) times 1/3 (expected


mistakes)=1/9. 2/3 (A and C, different color)


times 1 (expected mistakes) = 2/3. Now


1/9+2/3=7/9.
